Import from any other platform

For a platform Roundturn has no reader for, an AI chatbot such as ChatGPT or Claude turns your platform's export into Roundturn's own import file. Roundturn then checks every row, and you check the total against your statement, before anything is saved.

The file
roundturn-import.csv, made by the chatbot
In Roundturn
Other platform (converted with an AI chatbot)
The check
Your statement's net P&L

  1. On your platform

    Export your trades, and note the net P&L

    Export your trade history for the dates you want, in whatever form your platform gives: fills, orders or trades, as a CSV, a spreadsheet or a text file. Then note the net P&L your platform's statement shows for the same dates, after commissions and fees. That number is how you and Roundturn know the chatbot's file is right.

  2. In Roundturn

    Choose Other platform, and copy the prompt

    On the Import page, choose the Roundturn account the trades belong to, then Choose files. Roundturn asks where the trades are from: pick Other platform (converted with an AI chatbot).

    Type the net P&L from your statement, then click Copy prompt. See the prompt shows it first if you want to read it.

  3. In your AI chatbot

    Paste the prompt, attach your export, and answer its questions

    Open ChatGPT, Claude or another chatbot that reads attached files. Paste the prompt and attach your export. The chatbot asks only what the file does not say for certain: the time zone of its times, whether a row is a fill or a whole trade, your fees if the file has none, and which account, if there are several.

    Before it gives you the file, it shows a summary: the number of round turns, the dates, and the total gross P&L, fees and net P&L. Check the net P&L against your statement. If they differ, ask it to find out why before going on.

    Then download roundturn-import.csv. If the chatbot shows the file as text instead, copy it into a plain text file and save it with that name.

  4. In Roundturn

    Choose the file, check, and import

    Choose file opens your computer's file window: choose roundturn-import.csv. The preview shows the round turns, the days they cover, and their gross P&L, fees and net P&L, beside the figure you typed: either matches your statement, or how far apart the two are.

    Rows that do not add up are listed by line and left out; the rest can be imported. Nothing is saved until you click Import, and every import can be undone from the import history.

What Roundturn checks

  • The file is Roundturn's import file, version 1: exactly its eleven columns, with 1 as the version on every row. Anything else, your platform's own export included, is refused whole, with the reason.
  • Every row: a contract Roundturn knows, long or short, a whole number of contracts, times with their UTC offset, an exit no earlier than the entry and not in the future, and, for one contract, prices that are a whole number of ticks.
  • The money: the row's gross P&L must be what its prices make (the price move × the contracts × the contract's dollars per point), to the cent, and its net P&L must be gross minus fees. This catches a micro written as a mini, a long written as a short, or a misread price.
  • Trades already imported are recognised and left alone, even when the chatbot writes them a little differently the next time. So you can ask it to fix a refused row and import the corrected file: only that row is added.
  • The total: the file's net P&L against your statement's. The import waits until you type the figure. If they differ, the preview says by how much; you can still import if you know why.

The file, column by column

The prompt tells the chatbot all of this. It is here so you can check its work, or write the file yourself. One row per round turn: a position in one contract, from flat back to flat.

roundturn_version,symbol,side,qty,entry_time,entry_price,exit_time,exit_price,gross_pnl,fees,net_pnl
1,NQZ6,long,2,2026-11-18T09:31:05-05:00,21000.25,2026-11-18T09:33:10-05:00,21010.5,410.00,9.00,401.00
1,MESZ6,short,1,2026-11-18T10:01:00-05:00,6001.25,2026-11-18T10:05:00-05:00,6000.00,6.25,1.24,5.01
roundturn_version
1 on every row.
symbol
The contract as Tradovate writes it: the exchange's root, the month code and the year, so NQZ6 is December 2026's E-mini Nasdaq-100. The root alone (NQ) when the export does not show the month.
side
long or short.
qty
The contracts bought in the round turn, which is also the contracts sold.
entry_time
The first opening fill, with its UTC offset: 2026-11-18T09:31:05-05:00.
entry_price
The average price of the opening fills, not rounded, in the exchange's units.
exit_time
The fill that made the position flat, written like entry_time.
exit_price
The average price of the closing fills.
gross_pnl
Dollars before fees, negative for a loss.
fees
The round turn's commissions and fees, both sides together, zero or more.
net_pnl
gross_pnl minus fees.

Good to know

  • Your export goes to the chatbot you choose, under that company's own terms. Roundturn never sends your file to an AI, and uses none itself: it only reads the file the chatbot gives you. You can blank out account numbers before you attach the export.
  • Fees come from each account's own commission setting, charged on every trade, as for every import. The preview shows the file's fees beside your setting and offers to use them.
  • Times carry their own UTC offset, so the file says its own time zone. The preview shows them in that offset, or in your CSV time zone setting when the file has more than one (across a daylight saving change).
  • A position still open at the end of the export is not a round turn yet. The chatbot leaves it out; import it after it is closed.
  • One file per import, up to 10 MB and 50,000 round turns.

If the preview says

"This is not Roundturn's import file."
The file is not the chatbot's, most likely your platform's own export. Choose roundturn-import.csv, or start a new chat with the prompt.
"The header row is not Roundturn's import file version 1"
The chatbot renamed, added or left out a column. Ask it to use exactly the header row the prompt gives, with no other columns.
"roundturn_version is '2', and Roundturn reads version 1"
The file was made for another version of the format. Copy the prompt again from the Import page and start a new chat.
"gross_pnl … is not what the prices make"
The contract, the side, the contracts or a price is wrong in that row: often a micro written as a mini, or a platform's own code for a contract. Ask the chatbot to check that round turn against your export, then import the corrected file.
"… has no UTC offset"
Ask the chatbot to write every time with its offset, like 2026-11-18T09:31:05-05:00.
"the contract … is not in Roundturn's contract list"
The chatbot wrote the platform's own code (CQG writes EP for ES), or Roundturn does not know the contract yet. Ask for the exchange's root; if it is right, tell us the contract.
"The file's net P&L is …, but your statement says …"
A trade may be missing, doubled or wrong, the fees may differ, or the statement covers other dates. Ask the chatbot to compare the file with your export row by row.
